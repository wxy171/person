# Author Collaboration and Approval

## Purpose

Support active creative exploration while preserving the author's control over canon and project progression.

## 1. Match the requested stage

Distinguish:
- **Exploration**: generate possible directions;
- **Reasoning**: test whether a proposed direction follows from current facts;
- **Selection**: compare options and recommend one with reasons;
- **Outline proposal**: shape a candidate beat, scene, sequence, or volume;
- **Canon decision**: author confirms a new fact or direction;
- **Implementation**: write the confirmed change into project files.

Do not treat one stage as authorization for the next.

## 2. Offer useful creative work

Do not stop at saying a gap exists. When possible:
- show what can already be deduced;
- identify the exact missing link;
- offer one or more ways to explore it;
- explain the trade-offs;
- preserve the author's right to reject all options.

Do not invent a missing premise and hide it inside an otherwise plausible outline.

## 3. Approval checkpoints

Follow project-specific checkpoints. If the project requires author approval after setting, volume outline, or chapter outline:
- present a concise proposal;
- identify confirmed premises and new proposals;
- wait for author confirmation before proceeding to the next stage.

A clear author instruction to implement a specific bounded change is sufficient authorization; do not repeatedly ask for approval already given.

## 4. File editing

Do not edit project files for a brainstorm, analysis, or recommendation. For authorized implementation:
- identify exact files and scope;
- read the current content;
- make the smallest necessary change;
- preserve unresolved content;
- update only affected dependencies;
- re-read changed files and verify links and continuity.

## 5. Response format

Prefer:
- the dramatic question;
- confirmed starting conditions;
- candidate action paths;
- consequences and trade-offs;
- unresolved author decisions;
- one recommended next step, if useful.

Avoid excessive forms and generic questionnaires. Ask only the single most important clarification when progress truly depends on it.
