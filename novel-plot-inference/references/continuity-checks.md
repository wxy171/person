# Continuity Checks for Plot Design

## Purpose

Ensure a proposed plot path can coexist with the established story, without turning continuity checking into the primary creative task.

## 1. Check only relevant sources

Read the project instructions and story index, then inspect sources relevant to the proposed characters, events, rules, timeline, mysteries, and scene location. Follow the project's own authority rules.

Do not claim a project-wide audit after checking only a subset.

## 2. Character continuity

For each consequential action, check:
- identity and current status;
- knowledge at that exact point in story time;
- beliefs and misunderstandings;
- established goals and commitments;
- abilities, resources, and restrictions;
- relationship status from the relevant perspectives.

A character may act irrationally, but the story should establish the reason or portray the irrationality intentionally. Do not invent a rationale to conceal a gap.

## 3. Timeline continuity

Check:
- event order;
- travel and access time, if established;
- character presence or absence;
- information acquisition order;
- reveal restrictions;
- duration of unresolved conditions.

Do not calculate missing dates or durations without sufficient inputs.

## 4. World-rule continuity

For every rule used by the proposed plot:
- identify its governing source;
- confirm its status and scope;
- check the trigger and result actually stated;
- separate character explanation from objective rule;
- identify any missing interaction as unresolved.

Do not create a rule to make a desired plot path possible.

## 5. Relationship continuity

Track what each person knows, believes, remembers, or acknowledges about the relationship. Do not infer kinship, romance, friendship, allegiance, or hostility from proximity or convenience.

## 6. Mystery and foreshadowing continuity

Check whether:
- the clue has been introduced;
- its timing is allowed;
- the character has access to it;
- the planned reveal is authorized by existing evidence;
- the reveal timing conflicts with any project restriction;
- a supposedly unresolved mystery has already been explicitly answered or deprecated.

## 7. Finding labels

Classify each issue as:
- direct contradiction;
- possible contradiction;
- timeline gap;
- knowledge-boundary violation;
- unsupported causal link;
- stale/deprecated material;
- unresolved dependency;
- no issue found within the inspected scope.

## 8. Report scope honestly

Name the files and sections checked. List inaccessible or missing sources that materially limit the result. Do not infer their contents from references to them.
