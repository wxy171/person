# Character Management

## Purpose

Maintain consistency in character identity, history, goals, knowledge, decisions, behavior, and relationships without inventing biography or reducing a character to a static profile.

## 1. Read before editing

Before answering a character question or changing a character file, inspect:

- the character's primary profile, if one exists;
- project-level character indexes and relationship references;
- relevant timeline entries;
- scenes or summaries that establish important actions and knowledge;
- other characters' files when they contain claims about this character;
- project instructions about which source governs character facts.

Do not assume that every project uses one file per character or a fixed folder layout.

## 2. Separate character information types

When supported by the source, distinguish:

- **Identity**: name, aliases, role, and explicitly established identity facts.
- **Background**: family, origin, education, prior events, and other history.
- **Current state**: location, status, condition, affiliations, and current circumstances.
- **Goals**: stated aims, inferred aims, immediate needs, and longer-term intentions.
- **Knowledge**: what the character knows, does not know, remembers, suspects, or misunderstands.
- **Beliefs and interpretations**: what the character thinks is true; this may differ from objective canon.
- **Actions**: what the character actually did, as distinct from what they intended or claimed.
- **Relationships**: explicitly established connections and their current state.
- **Unresolved matters**: intentionally open or conflicting details.

Do not fill empty fields merely to make a profile look complete.

## 3. Track knowledge precisely

For consequential information, ask:

- What information was available to the character?
- When and by what means did they obtain it?
- Was it directly observed, reported, inferred, remembered, or misunderstood?
- Is the knowledge current at the scene's point in time?
- Has the character forgotten, concealed, misinterpreted, or revised it?
- Does the narration establish objective truth, or only the character's belief?

Do not give a character knowledge solely because the reader or another character knows it. Do not treat suspicion as confirmation.

## 4. Test motivation and action

When checking whether an action is plausible, compare it with:

- the character's established goals and immediate stakes;
- knowledge available at that moment;
- constraints and resources actually established;
- prior decisions and commitments;
- relevant emotional or relational context, if documented.

A surprising action is not automatically inconsistent. Identify the missing motivation or transition as a question; do not invent a motive to make it work.

## 5. Manage relationships without fabrication

- Record only relationships supported by an explicit source or author confirmation.
- Do not infer family ties, romance, friendship, mentorship, rivalry, allegiance, or shared history from names, proximity, resemblance, or narrative convenience.
- Do not force relationships to be reciprocal in the data model. A relationship can be perceived, known, or recorded differently by each party.
- Distinguish objective relationship status from each character's understanding of it.
- Track changes over time where the source supports them.
- When two character files disagree, inspect perspective and chronology before calling it a contradiction.
- If reciprocity or relationship status is not established, mark it unresolved rather than completing the pair.

## 6. Timeline and identity continuity

Check age, sequence, presence, absence, role, and identity claims against the project timeline. Do not calculate missing dates or ages unless the inputs and calculation rule are explicit. If two timelines differ, use the project's authority rule; otherwise report the conflict.

## 7. Editing checklist

Before saving a character change:

- [ ] Every new fact has an identifiable source or explicit author authorization.
- [ ] Knowledge is assigned only from information available at the relevant time.
- [ ] Belief, suspicion, memory, and objective fact remain distinct.
- [ ] No relationship or family connection was invented.
- [ ] Relevant linked character files and indexes were checked.
- [ ] Timeline consequences were checked.
- [ ] Unresolved fields remain unresolved.
- [ ] Unrelated character details were left untouched.
