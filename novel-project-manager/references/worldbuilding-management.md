# Worldbuilding and Mechanism Management

## Purpose

Analyze fictional rules and systems for completeness, consistency, and clarity without silently adding mechanics.

## 1. Discover the governing sources

Search for the rule's name, aliases, earlier names, related processes, exceptions, and references. Read the governing definition and the relevant dependent sections. Follow the project's own authority rules.

Do not assume a worldbuilding system must use a particular taxonomy, layer count, metaphysics, or document structure.

## 2. Extract the rule as written

Where relevant, represent a rule using these fields. Mark fields not stated by the source as "not specified"; do not fill them by convention.

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Definition | What does the concept mean? |
| Scope | Which entities, events, places, or states does it apply to? |
| Preconditions | What must already be true? |
| Trigger | What causes the rule to operate? |
| Process | What happens, and in what order? |
| Outcome | What changes, and what does not change? |
| Constraints | What limits the operation? |
| Exceptions | Which exceptions are explicitly established? |
| Dependencies | Which other rules or concepts does it rely on? |
| Observability | What can characters or observers perceive? |
| Reversibility | Can the result be undone, and under what stated conditions? |
| Status | Confirmed, unresolved, inferred, or deprecated under project conventions |

Not every rule needs every field. The table is an analysis aid, not a demand to add missing mechanics.

## 3. Test logical structure

Check for:

- circular definitions where a concept is explained only through itself;
- undefined terms used as if already defined;
- a trigger that does not connect to the stated process;
- an outcome that does not follow from the described process;
- hidden prerequisites or unspoken actors;
- scope changes between definition and example;
- incompatible claims about permanence, reversibility, or conservation;
- exceptions that contradict the general rule without a stated boundary;
- two distinct concepts being used interchangeably;
- a rule that depends on another rule whose status is unresolved.

Describe the exact logical gap. Do not patch it by inventing a threshold, exception, trigger, actor, cost, or hidden condition.

## 4. Separate mechanism from interpretation

Keep distinct:

- the rule as objectively defined by the setting;
- what a character observes;
- what a character believes the rule means;
- what an organization or source claims;
- what the reader may infer;
- what remains unknown to the author or intentionally unrevealed.

An unreliable explanation is not automatically the world's actual rule. A character's misunderstanding does not rewrite canon.

## 5. Analyze interactions between rules

For each interaction, state:

1. which rules are involved;
2. the exact clauses that connect them;
3. whether the interaction is explicitly defined or inferred;
4. the resulting consistency issue, if any;
5. what additional decision would be needed.

Do not infer that two rules interact merely because they share a word or appear in the same chapter.

## 6. Report completeness honestly

Use precise labels:

- **Defined**: the source states the relevant behavior.
- **Partially defined**: some necessary aspects are specified, others are not.
- **Ambiguous**: wording supports more than one interpretation.
- **Inconsistent**: sources or clauses cannot all hold under the same stated scope.
- **Unspecified**: no source establishes the behavior.
- **Inferred**: a possible consequence, not confirmed.

Do not call a rule "complete" merely because it has a definition, trigger, and outcome. Completeness depends on what the story needs to rely on.

## 7. Editing checklist

- [ ] Governing definition and related rules were read.
- [ ] Existing terms were preserved or deliberately revised with authorization.
- [ ] Every added condition or outcome is sourced or author-approved.
- [ ] Inferences are labelled and not inserted as facts.
- [ ] Rule interactions and dependent references were checked.
- [ ] Examples do not accidentally broaden the rule's scope.
- [ ] Unspecified behavior remains visibly unspecified.
