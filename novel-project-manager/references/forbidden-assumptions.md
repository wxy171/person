# Forbidden Assumptions and Failure Modes

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Do not invent canon.** No unsupported facts, causes, motives, identities, relationships, mechanisms, chronology, or outcomes.
2. **Do not promote inference.** A plausible deduction remains an inference until the author or the project's authority system establishes it.
3. **Do not complete missing rules by convention.** Never add thresholds, costs, triggers, exceptions, actors, prerequisites, limits, or reversibility conditions merely because a system seems incomplete.
4. **Do not invent relationships.** Proximity, shared scenes, similar names, resemblance, and narrative convenience do not establish kinship, romance, friendship, rivalry, or allegiance.
5. **Do not invent character knowledge.** A character cannot act on information they have not acquired unless the project establishes another way they could know it.
6. **Do not conflate perspectives.** A character's belief, memory, report, or interpretation is not automatically objective truth.
7. **Do not impose a universal source hierarchy.** Follow the project's explicit authority rules. If none resolves a conflict, report it.
8. **Do not use recency as automatic authority.** Newer timestamps, filenames, or document order do not by themselves supersede older sources.
9. **Do not revive deprecated concepts.** Mention them only when explaining history, migration, or an intentional quotation.
10. **Do not perform blind terminology replacement.** Inspect every occurrence in context.
11. **Do not treat silence as negation.** An undocumented fact is unspecified, not necessarily false.
12. **Do not turn proposals into facts.** Keep brainstorms, options, and possible repairs visibly separate from canon.
13. **Do not silently resolve contradictions.** Present evidence and let the project's rules or the author decide.
14. **Do not edit without authorization.** Analysis, diagnosis, and recommendations are not permission to change files.
15. **Do not rewrite unrelated content.** Avoid opportunistic cleanup and stylistic refactoring outside the task.
16. **Do not claim unperformed work.** Never claim to have read, searched, tested, validated, uploaded, or changed something unless it actually happened.

## Common reasoning traps

### Filling a blank profile
A missing field is not an invitation to create a biography. Leave it unspecified or ask.

### Making every relationship reciprocal
One person's belief or record of a relationship may differ from the other's. Do not force symmetry unless the project requires it.

### Treating a summary as the source
Summaries can omit qualifications. Read the governing source before changing a definition.

### Treating every discrepancy as an error
Time, perspective, scope, unreliable information, and deliberate uncertainty can explain differences. Test these possibilities before classifying a conflict.

### Treating every discrepancy as intentional mystery
A mystery does not excuse broken causal logic or incompatible definitions. Report what the text establishes and what it leaves open.

### Solving a logical gap by adding a mechanic
First state the missing link. Offer alternative repairs only as proposals and explain what each would change.

### Rewriting for apparent completeness
Do not add headings, fields, indexes, or metadata just to make documents uniform. Follow the existing project conventions unless asked to redesign them.

## Safe fallback

When evidence is insufficient, use a direct statement such as:

- "The available files do not establish this."
- "These two sources conflict; I found no project rule that resolves the conflict."
- "This is a possible interpretation, not confirmed canon."
- "The rule does not specify this condition."
- "This change would require an author decision."

Accuracy is more important than producing a complete-looking answer.
